It must NEVER be a real relative's voice and NEVER a cloned voice** (decision 8, `docs/process/decision-log.md`: drills use stock voices in v1; cloned family voices come only after a consent study). ## What this clip is for

- It is shown in the pitch and in the clickable screens as the "Practice call" a senior would hear. It is fiction: the caller, the accident, the amount and the "PayNow" are made up, and no real person, bank, police force or phone number is named.
- It is **not** a test on grandparents. The interview guide forbids realistic scam simulations without separate, explicit consent and a human from the team present (`docs/research/interview-guide.md`, "No realistic scam simulation without consent"). Do not play this clip in a V2 session unless the team has that consent and that person in the room.

## The 60-second call

The caller is a fictional grandson phoning "Ah Ma" from a new number. Each line carries cues the Scam Check engine also looks for (`docs/prototype-design.md`, cue table): identity switch, urgency, authority, money request, secrecy, emotional pressure.

Delivery: a young, hurried, slightly shaky voice that stays plausible, not a cartoon villain. The senior does not speak in the recording; the short pauses are where she would answer.

| Time | Who | What is said (say it exactly; "new number", "now" and "don't tell anyone" are the phrases the debrief quotes) | Cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 0:59 to 1:00 | Sound | Line stays open, faint room noise. The clip stops and waits for her choice | |

Checked by script on 23 Oct 2026: the caller speaks **120 words**; at 2.4 words a second (about 144 a minute, a hurried but clear pace) that is about 50 seconds, plus 3 s of ring and three pauses of 2 s each (6 s), plus a 1 s tail, so the clip runs about 59 to 60 seconds. Timings are estimates until a real take is timed (see "Recording tips").

Why these lines:

- "I lost my phone, this is my new number", "now" and "don't tell anyone" are the same phrases the debrief quotes, so what the senior hears is what the debrief names.
- "Papa" is deliberately a person the senior would normally call. The debrief can point out that the caller wanted her **not** to tell family, which is exactly why the app's button calls Wei Jie.
- No digits, account names or numbers are spoken ("I'll message you the number"). Do not add a real-looking number or a real bank, police or government name.

## Debrief lines

The red flags were:", the four flags, "3 drills this month", "Wei Jie sees your progress, never the call audio." The lines below add a short spoken or captioned voice-over. Tone rule: praise, never blame, and never the word "safe".

| Outcome | Voice-over (about 9 to 14 s) |
| --- | --- |
| She pressed "Is this really them?" | "That was a practice call. You pressed 'Is this really them?' Well done. Real family do not ask you to keep money a secret. If it happens for real, call Wei Jie on his saved number." |
| She hung up | "That was a practice call. You hung up, and no money moved. Next time, press 'Is this really them?' to call Wei Jie." |
| The clip ended with no choice | "The call ended before you chose. In a real call, press 'Is this really them?' or hang up, then call Wei Jie yourself." |

Red flags to show, in the order they are heard. The first four are already on screen 5; the last two are optional:

1. A new number ("this is my new number")
2. Asking for money ("I need three thousand dollars")
3. Rushing you ("now", "in ten minutes")
4. Keep it secret ("don't tell anyone")
5. Optional: pressure on your feelings ("I'm scared")
6. Optional: a threat of police or trouble

Guardian side (unchanged from the design doc): Wei Jie sees that the drill was done and how quickly she chose, never the audio.

Spoken languages: the clip is English with Singapore touches ("Ah Ma"). Mandarin, Malay and Tamil versions need native speakers, both to voice them and to check the wording (decision 18). Until then show the English clip with captions.

## Recording tips

1. **Where:** a small, quiet room with soft furnishings (a wardrobe or a duvet over your head works). Switch off fans and air-conditioning if you can, and put the phone on silent.
2. **What with:** the phone's voice-memo app, about a hand's width (15 cm) from the mouth, a little to the side to avoid pops. Save as WAV or M4A, not a heavily compressed format, then export an MP3 for the prototype.
3. **Read the script twice before recording.** Aim for a hurried but clear pace (about 144 words a minute). Do not shout and do not do a "movie villain" voice: real scam callers sound ordinary and in a hurry.
4. **Record three takes** of the whole call, leaving the pauses in the table (2 s, 2 s, 2 s). Pick the best, then time it: it should land at 55 to 65 seconds. If it is longer, cut the "Are you at the bank?" line first.
5. **Phone-call feel (optional):** a mild filter that removes the very low and very high frequencies makes it sound like a phone line. Keep it light and keep every word clear. Do not add music.
6. **Check the words:** play it back and tick every line of the table. The phrases "new number", "now" and "don't tell anyone" must be clearly audible.
7. **Safety and privacy:** no real names, numbers or bank names. Do not upload the file to cloud AI tools or post it anywhere public. Keep the signed consent form with the file.
8. **File names:** `drill-clip.mp3` for the prototype and `drill-clip-master.wav` for the master. Do not commit the audio to the public repo unless the teammate ticked the public box and the team agrees (decision 14: repo stays public, so the default is to keep audio out).
9. **Re-time after the real take** and update the time column above, because the caption timings depend on it.

## How it plugs into the clickable screens

The AI did not edit `design/clickable-screens/` (another folder). This section tells the Designer what to change.

**Today:** screen 4 shows "Practice call", "This is not a real call", an unknown number and the script as text ("You hear: Ma, it's me. I lost my phone, this is my new number. I need money now. Don't tell anyone."). "Is this really them?" and "Hang up" both lead to screen 5, which shows "Well done for pausing" and which button was pressed.

**With the clip:**

| Step | What happens | Where in `design/clickable-screens/index.html` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Entering screen 4 (a tap on the picker or "Practise again" counts as the click browsers need) starts the audio from 0:00 | the function that shows a screen (`go`), and the `data-go="s-drill"` buttons |
| 2 | The "You hear:" box shows the lines as captions, one at a time, using the times in the table above, so the clip is never audio-only | the `.hear` paragraph on screen 4 |
| 3 | "Is this really them?" stops the audio with a 0.3 s fade and opens screen 5 with the "pressed" wording | `data-action="drill-pause"` and its case in the click handler |
| 4 | "Hang up" stops the audio and opens screen 5 with "You hung up." | `data-action="drill-hangup"` |
| 5 | If the clip reaches 1:00 with no choice, open screen 5 with the third wording ("The call ended before you chose") | a new `ended` handler on the audio |
| 6 | Screen 5 plays or shows the matching voice-over from the debrief table | screen 5 text |

Fixes the clip makes visible:

- **Heading on screen 5.** It says "Well done for pausing" whatever she pressed, even "You hung up." Use a heading that matches the outcome (for the hang-up and no-choice cases, for example "Practice call finished").
- **Wording match.** The on-screen script says "Ma, it's me"; the clip says "Ah Ma ... your grandson". Replace the on-screen text with the clip's captions so the screen, the audio and the flags agree. The English line "Keep it secret: don't tell anyone" already matches the clip.
- **Seconds taken.** The design doc records how fast she pressed the button (`secondsToPause`). With the audio, use its current time, and consider "You paused after 23 seconds" on screen 5 and in what Wei Jie sees.

A minimal sketch of the audio hook (not tested, not added to any file):

```js
var clip = new Audio("drill-clip.mp3");              // a local file next to the page; no CDN
function startDrill() { clip.currentTime = 0; clip.play(); }
function stopDrill() { var s = Math.round(clip.currentTime); clip.pause(); return s; }   // seconds taken
clip.addEventListener("ended", function () { /* open screen 5 with the "no choice" wording */ });
```

Keep the page working offline as one file where possible. A 60-second MP3 is about 0.5 to 1 MB; if the page must stay a single file, the audio can be embedded as a data URI (about a third larger), at the cost of a heavier page. Decide with the Dev who owns the demo.

In the pitch: show screen 4, press play, let the audience hear lines 1 to 5, press "Is this really them?" at about 0:45, then show screen 5 and the guardian's side. That is about 60 to 75 seconds including the voice-over.
