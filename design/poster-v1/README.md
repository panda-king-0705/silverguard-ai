# Poster v1 (backlog item P4, issue #4)

> Draft prepared with AI assistance for the Designer; to be reviewed and edited by the team.

`index.html` is the A3 portrait poster (297 × 420 mm) with the wireframe's 11 zones filled with real content. It is one offline file (system fonts, no CDNs) that uses two diagram PNGs from `docs/diagrams/`, copied here unchanged and referenced relatively. It prints as one A3 page through `@page`. Owner: Designer, with the PO. It follows decisions 11 to 13 and 20 in `docs/process/decision-log.md`.

## How to use it

- Open `index.html` in Chrome or Edge. On screen the poster is scaled to the window; a status line shows the body size the page chose.
- Print: Ctrl/Cmd+P, paper A3, scale 100%, margins none, "Background graphics" on, then "Save as PDF".
- A small script fits the text. It starts at 20 pt body text (decision 12) and steps down by 0.25 pt until nothing overflows, so one body size is used on the whole poster. If a zone still overflows at 12 pt, it gets a red outline and the status line says so.
- Placeholders are dashed amber boxes. Everything that needs a human is listed under "Open items" below.
- Checks: `python3 checks/check_poster_v1.py` (add `--words-only` to skip the browser, `--shot poster.png` to save a picture). It uses the locally installed Playwright and Chromium and downloads nothing.

## What fits: finding for the team (decision 12 does not hold)

Decision 12 says about 276 words at 20 pt body. With the real text, the real headline, two diagrams and the QR box, **20 pt does not fit**. The wireframe's density test measured filler words of about 3 letters, empty diagram boxes and no headline of 7 words, so it was optimistic.

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| First browser run of `check_poster_v1.py` | Body text fitted at 13.5 pt, but in that run two diagram boxes were being squashed by a layout bug, so the true figure was lower |
| After three fixes (image height, stats overlap, no squashing) | Not re-measured, because the brief allows one browser run. A font-metric calculation (Inter, the system font here) estimates about **13.5 to 14 pt**: it fits at 13.5 pt, overshoots by about 2 mm at 14 pt, 26 mm at 16 pt, 52 mm at 18 pt and 86 mm at 20 pt (page height 387 mm after margins) |
| Without JavaScript | Falls back to 13 pt, which the estimate says fits |

**Run `python3 checks/check_poster_v1.py` once to get the real number.** The script keeps the poster inside the page whatever the machine's fonts, so nothing overflows; only the size changes.

Choices for the team (the AI has not made them):

1. **Accept about 14 pt.** Fine on an A3 PDF read on a screen or at arm's length; weak for a poster read at 1 m (the wireframe's table puts 14 pt at 0.74 m).
2. **Cut about 26 mm of height to reach 16 pt.** Cheapest cut: drop the flow crop in zone 4 (about 24 mm). Alternatively shrink both crops, but their text is then too small to read.
3. **Reach 20 pt.** The estimate is 86 mm over at 20 pt, more than a whole zone row, so it would mean dropping a diagram and several zones' text.

## Colour and contrast

Teal marks protection (zone 4 and headings), indigo marks Scam Check (zone 5), red is used only for risk and always has words and an icon (the High risk chip, zone 10), amber marks anything that needs a human. "No warning signs found" is a neutral grey chip with a dashed ring, not green, so colour never reads as "safe". The browser check measures every text element in the first run: **81 elements, lowest 5.52:1** (amber brand on teal), all at least 4.5:1. Colours changed afterwards only in size, not value.
