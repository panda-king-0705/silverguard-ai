## 3. Claims the poster must never make

1. **"SPF says AI voice-clone scams are rising."** SPF's 2025 and 2026 briefs don't mention voice cloning or deepfakes used by scammers. Voice-clone evidence comes from GovTech (A1), the FTC (A4), the FBI IC3 (A7), CBC (A9) and Sobolev (A11). Write "Singapore Police Force figures show…" only for scam counts, losses and age groups.
2. **"Seniors are more susceptible to scams."** Buse et al. (2024) found no age effect, so don't cite it for that. The evidence is on losses per victim (rows 4 and 5), not susceptibility.
3. **"Banks must refund voice-clone scam victims."** The Shared Responsibility Framework (B14) is about a defined set of phishing scams; its scope for family-emergency transfers is not checked.
4. **"Safe."** SilverGuard never says a call or message is safe. Its levels are "High risk", "Check with family" and "No warning signs found".
5. **"The cloned voice is detected."** The product detects the scam script (money request, secrecy, new number, urgency, authority, emotional pressure), not the voice.
