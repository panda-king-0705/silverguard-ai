## How to run a rehearsal

- **Timekeeper:** the SM. Start the stopwatch on Speaker A's first word; write the clock time at the **end** of each beat (the moment the next speaker starts). Do not stop the watch for mistakes: the real pitch will not stop either.
- **Run 1 (Sat morning):** cue cards allowed, demo in Mock mode. Goal: find the slow beats.
- **Run 2 (Sat afternoon):** deck and demo, no script in hand, cue cards only. Practise **one planned fallback** (the SM names it without warning, for example "the alert did not arrive").
- **Run 3 (Sun):** full dress run, then the mock-investor Q&A (two drill rounds, section 4).
- After each run, 5 minutes of feedback: each speaker gets one "keep" and one "fix". Write the fixes on the cue cards.
- **Pass mark:** finish between **9:00 and 9:45**. Over 9:45: cut in the script's order (1) the vendor-quote sentence in beat 13, (2) the independence sentence in beat 10, (3) the Deloitte sentence in beat 11, (4) the Google Pixel sentence in beat 10.

## 3. Fallback decision tree

Rule for every branch: **never spend more than 20 seconds recovering, and always say out loud which fallback you are using.** Being honest about a fallback costs nothing; pretending costs credibility.

### Live alert fails (beat 6)

```
D presses "Simulate unknown call"
│
├─ Message shows on the phone (or Telegram Web) within 12 s ──► F reads it. Carry on.
│
└─ Nothing after 12 s
   │  D: "It is on its way."
   ├─ 1. Telegram Web window (if it was set up): look there for 5 s.
   │     Arrived ──► F reads it from the laptop. Carry on.
   │
   ├─ 2. Mock mode: tick Mock mode on, press again.
   │     D: "The network is slow, so here is the same alert in mock mode: it is simulated, not sent."
   │     Works ──► F reads it from the page ("What Marcus receives"). Carry on.
   │
   ├─ 3. Page frozen or will not open: show clickable screen 6.
   │     D: "This is what the product sends."
   │
   └─ 4. Laptop or browser unusable: play the backup video.
         D: "This is the recorded run."
```

If the live go was **not** given, start at step 2: run Mock mode from the start, say "Watch the screen" (not "Watch this phone"), and say once that the alert is simulated, not sent.

### Scam Check gives an unexpected result (beat 7)

- Wrong level: "This demo is simple word rules, so the wording matters; the product adds a trained model." Move on. Do not retype.
- Nothing happens: press Clear, paste once more. Still nothing: show clickable screen 3 (same High risk card), then the backup video if needed.

### Projector fails

1. The SM checks the cable and input once (no more than 20 s).
2. Speakers carry on from the cue cards; point to the **poster** for the numbers (beats 2, 3, 11).
3. Demos: D and E turn the laptop to face the investors and run the demo on its screen.
4. If the room is too big for that, play the backup video on the laptop screen or on a phone held up, and say "this is the recorded run".

### Laptop fails

1. Spare laptop (if the team has one): USB stick, open the deck PDF and the three demo pages from the stick (they work offline from disk).
2. No spare laptop: carry on spoken from the cue cards with the poster; play the backup video from the phone for the demo beats ("this is the recorded run").
3. The live alert is skipped in this branch; do not try to set up a token on a borrowed machine.

### Someone freezes or is absent

- The speaker's backup (agreed at planning) has a copy of the same cue cards. Suggested pairs: A and E (both say the S$500,000), B and C (both carry evidence), D and F (both run demos).
- A speaker who blanks looks at the "Say exactly" box on the card, says the numbers, and hands over.

## 4. Q&A drill

Uses the questions in the [Q&A bank](../pitch/qa-bank.md) (20 questions, plus the price and terms answers the PO's AI is adding on 6 Nov; the script refers to the terms answer as Q23, so check the numbering when it lands). Run it after rehearsal 2 (round 1) and after rehearsal 3 (round 2). About 25 minutes per round.

**Rules**

- Answers are **30 seconds**. The SM times them and calls "time" at 30 s.
- Owners answer in **their own words**, without the bank in hand. The first sentence must answer the question.
- The firer plays a sceptical investor: interrupt once if the answer drifts.
- After each answer, the firer scores it (rubric below) and gives one line of feedback. 30 s of feedback, then the next question.
- **Price and terms always go to the PO**, whoever is asked first: "That is one for our product owner."

### Scoring rubric (firer scores, 0–2 each, out of 10)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct** | Never answers the question | Answers it, but after a long run-up | First sentence answers it |
| **Time** | Over 40 s | 31–40 s | 30 s or less |
| **Numbers exact** | A wrong or invented number | Right number, vague wording ("about half a million") | Exact as in the script and bank, or no number needed |
| **Honest** | Overclaims (says "safe", "detects cloned voices", links SPF to voice cloning, offers equity or a valuation, promises a partner) | Hedges without saying the limit | States the limit plainly (the bank's "Careful" line) |
| **Own words** | Reads or recites the AI draft | Mostly the draft, some own phrasing | Clearly their own words, natural |

## 5. Retro after the pitch

Same day if possible, 20 minutes, in the format of [standup-and-retro.md](standup-and-retro.md) (silent notes under Keep, Drop, Try, then discuss). Record answers in the decision log (V4) as evidence; each person may draw on them for their own C3 reflection, which they write themselves.

1. What was our real time, and where did we lose or gain seconds against the stopwatch sheet?
2. Which demo path did we actually use (live, Telegram Web, mock, screen, video)? Was the switch smooth and did we say it out loud?
3. Which investor questions surprised us? Were any not in the Q&A bank?
4. Did anyone say a number differently from the script? Which one, and why?
5. Where did the price (S$8) and terms (open) answers land with the investors?
6. Which rehearsal habit helped most on the day: the stopwatch, the drill, the planned fallback, the cue cards?
7. Where did AI help this sprint, and where did we have to correct it? (List each correction for the V4 overrides table.)
8. What would we change if we had one more week?
9. What feedback did the instructor or the investors give, word for word if possible? (Keep instructor feedback in the private team space, not the public repo.)
10. Reminder, not a question: C2 peer evaluation and C3 individual reflection are each person's own work, written without AI.
